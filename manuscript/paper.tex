\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Mechanistic interpretability asks what a trained network \emph{computes}, but most
readouts answer it with proxies --- attention weights, saliency maps, activation
patches --- that are one step removed from the computation itself. We propose treating
the computation as what it already is: a \emph{graph}. GNOmE (Graph Networks f\textbf{O}r
\textbf{M}echanistic \textbf{E}xplicability) makes three moves. \textbf{Extract}: from a
trained MLP or transformer we build its \emph{circuit graph} --- one node per unit,
edges weighted by the blockwise mean-absolute Jacobian
$\mathbb{E}_x|\partial u_{k{+}1}/\partial u_k|$, thresholded into a layered DAG --- so
every edge is a real, measurable derivative rather than a heuristic proxy. Extraction
costs $\approx$1.4\% of training time. \textbf{Measure}: the extracted graph is scored
with ground-truth-free explicability metrics (sparsity, modularity, effective depth,
bottleneck, role diversity) and a composite Mechanistic Explicability Score, and ---
where the true circuit is known by construction (boolean DAGs, modular-arithmetic
Fourier circuits) --- \emph{circuit recovery}: how much of the ground-truth wiring a
trained model actually contains. Trained boolean models recover the full input-pair
co-wiring of their target circuit (recall 1.00 in 9/9 models; F1 0.50--0.90), and the
extracted graphs carry clean architectural signatures: on the same modular-arithmetic
task, MLPs wire graphs at sparsity 0.71 whereas transformers wire denser graphs at 0.43
and exhibit 2.3$\times$ higher role diversity. \textbf{Read}: a graph network (pure-PyTorch
GCN/GAT) reading the circuit graph infers unit depth-roles from structure alone --- no
positional features --- at macro-F1 0.91--0.93 from just four training graphs, versus 0.72
for a feature-only MLP baseline; the reading \emph{transfers to input sizes never seen
during training} (0.96 vs. 0.76 baseline) and fails, for every readout including the
baseline, across architectures (0.32, chance): structural explicability is
size-invariant but architecture-specific, and semantic content (recovery against an
unseen target) is not readable from structure at all. Mechanistic explicability is a
graph problem; GNOmE makes it measurable and learnable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A trained neural network is a function, but the function is implemented by a
\emph{wiring}: which units read which inputs, how deeply information flows, where
bottlenecks form. Mechanistic interpretability attempts to recover that wiring
\citep{olah2020zoom, elhage2021math, ferrando2024survey}. Its tools, however, largely
read \emph{proxies} for computation rather than computation itself: attention maps are
distributions over tokens, not paths through units \citep{jain2020attention}; saliency
and gradient attributions measure local sensitivity, not causal wiring
\citep{sundararajan2017axiomatic}; activation patching and circuit discovery
\citep{wang2022ioi, conmy2023acdc, zhang2023patching} infer edges through intervention,
which is powerful but expensive and heuristic in its thresholding.

The observation that underlies this paper is simpler: a model's computation \emph{is} a
graph, and the weights already determine it. Each unit's dependence on the previous
layer is a matrix of derivatives --- the Jacobian. Averaged over inputs and thresholded,
the Jacobians of a model's blocks define a layered directed acyclic graph: the
\emph{circuit graph}. Once the graph exists, three things become possible that are
otherwise hard. \emph{Measurement}: explicability becomes graph statistics --- sparsity,
modularity, effective depth, bottleneck width, role diversity --- all computable without
any ground truth, plus \emph{circuit recovery} where ground truth is available by
construction. \emph{Learning}: a \emph{graph network} \citep{battaglia2018relational,
sanchez2021gentle} is the natural readout of a graph, and can learn to read
explicability --- unit roles, depths, metric values --- that a feature-only model cannot
see, because depth and role are multi-hop quantities that message passing computes and
flat feature vectors cannot \citep{kipf2017gcn, velickovic2018gat}. And
\emph{boundary-setting}: because both positive and negative results are testable against
known ground-truth circuits, we can say precisely what a graph readout can and cannot
infer.

We make the case through three claims, each with committed, CPU-reproducible numbers.
First, the extraction is \emph{faithful}: on boolean circuits whose wiring is known by
construction, trained models' circuit graphs recover the target circuit's input-pair
co-wiring structure with recall 1.00 in all 9/9 trained models. Second, the extracted
graphs are \emph{informative}: explicability metrics separate architectures (MLP vs.\
transformer density and role diversity) and composite into a single score. Third, the
graphs are \emph{readable}: graph networks infer unit depth-roles few-shot from
structure alone and generalize to input sizes never seen during training --- while a
feature-only baseline does not --- and we identify a clean boundary: the same reading
does not transfer across architectures, and semantic recovery is not predictable from
structure by any readout. Mechanistic explicability, we conclude, is not a question
about features; it is a question about graphs, and graphs can be measured and read.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Extraction: circuit graphs from blockwise Jacobians}

GNOmE requires only that a model expose its \emph{blocks} --- differentiable maps
$u_k \mapsto u_{k+1}$ between unit layers --- and its unit dimensions. For each block
$k$ we compute the mean-absolute Jacobian
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:jac}
  \mathbf{W}_k[i,j] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x}\Big|\frac{\partial u_{k+1}[i]}{\partial u_k[j]}\Big|,
\end{equation}
estimated over a batch of inputs. Linear blocks have an input-independent Jacobian, so
they are computed exactly in closed form; elementwise blocks have a diagonal Jacobian
($|f'(u_j)|$ per unit); attention and MLP blocks fall back to one autograd pass per
output unit. Thresholding each layer at a multiple of its own mean nonzero edge weight
(``relative threshold'') yields a layered DAG whose edges are the computational paths
that actually fire (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Residual connections are folded into the
block they close, so the graph stays layered and every edge is a real path.

  differentiated blockwise to obtain mean-absolute Jacobians; per-layer relative
  thresholding produces a layered circuit graph; the graph is measured with

The extraction is cheap. Over the base benchmark (13 trained models spanning boolean
circuits and modular arithmetic, MLP and transformer), extraction took a mean of
0.49\,s per model (range 0.11--2.50\,s) against a mean training time of 34.4\,s ---
about 1.4\% of training cost. It is also faithful by construction: the edges are
derivatives of the actual computation, so a zero Jacobian means no computational path,
whatever attention or saliency might suggest.

\subsection{Measurement: explicability without ground truth}

Every extracted graph is scored with five graph-theoretic properties that require no
ground truth, each normalized so that \emph{higher is more explicable}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Sparsity} --- the fraction of possible layered edges that are absent.
        Dense wiring is harder to explain than sparse wiring.
  \item \textbf{Modularity} --- community structure of the undirected graph
        (Louvain-style greedy modularity). Modules are candidate functional units.
  \item \textbf{Effective depth} --- the longest directed path; how many genuinely
        sequential steps the computation takes.
  \item \textbf{Bottleneck} --- how much the graph narrows between input and output;
        a narrow middle implies a funnel of shared computation.
  \item \textbf{Role diversity} --- the fraction of distinct in/out-degree signatures;
        how many structurally distinct roles the units play.
\end{itemize}

These combine into a single Mechanistic Explicability Score
(MES; Methods), a weighted blend of recovery (when known), sparsity, modularity, and
depth fidelity.

\textbf{Architectural signatures.} On the same modular-arithmetic task, MLPs and
transformers produce measurably different circuit graphs (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}):
MLP sparsity 0.71--0.72 vs.\ transformer 0.41--0.44 --- transformers wire roughly twice
as dense a circuit graph --- and transformer role diversity (0.62 at $p{=}5$) is
$2.3\times$ the MLP's (0.27). Effective depth differs too: 5 for the MLP against 4 for
the one-layer transformer, whose attention is a single mixing step. These are the kind
of structural facts that \emph{measure} how a model computes rather than how well it
performs: two architectures at identical test accuracy (1.00) instantiate the same
function with different wiring, and the wiring is visible in the graph.

\subsection{Recovery: circuits known by construction}

Metrics without ground truth describe, but do not certify. GNOmE closes the loop with
tasks whose true circuits are known by construction: random layered DAGs of
\{AND, OR, XOR\} gates over $n_{\text{in}}$ inputs, and the canonical
Fourier/trigonometric circuit for modular addition \citep{nanda2023grokking,
nanda2022addition}. Recovery is \emph{functional}: for each pair of inputs, does some
unit co-wire them the way some ground-truth gate does? Precision/recall/F1 over input
pairs measures how faithfully the trained model reproduced the target circuit's
input-co-dependence.

The result is unambiguous. Across 9 trained boolean models (three gate counts $\times$
three seeds), every extracted graph contains the target circuit's input-pair wiring:
\textbf{recall 1.00 in 9/9 models}, mean F1 0.71 (range 0.50--0.90), mean precision
0.58 (range 0.33--0.81; Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery}). The only loss is precision: extracted
graphs contain \emph{extra} co-wiring beyond the minimal target --- redundant wiring is
the single measurable cost of learning a circuit in an overparameterized MLP. Recall
never fails: nothing the circuit needs is missing from the computation graph.

\subsection{Reading: graph networks learn to read explicability}

Extraction and measurement answer ``what does the wiring look like?''. The harder
question is ``what is a unit \emph{for}?'' --- a question about \emph{roles} that
structure alone should answer. We train graph networks (GCN \citep{kipf2017gcn} and GAT
\citep{velickovic2018gat}, implemented in pure PyTorch) to predict a node's
\emph{depth bin} --- input / early / mid / late / output --- from the circuit graph,
with node features deliberately restricted to degree and edge-weight statistics: no
layer index, no positional feature, so depth must be \emph{computed} by message passing
over the structure. A feature-only MLP baseline sees the same features with no edges.

\textbf{Few-shot role reading.} Training on just $K$ graphs and testing on held-out
graphs of the same task, graph networks read depth roles far above the baseline
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fewshot}). At $K{=}4$: GCN 0.91, GAT 0.89, vs.\ feature-only MLP 0.72
macro-F1. The gap persists across $K$ (Table~\ref{tab:exp1}): at every shot count the
graph readouts outperform the baseline by 0.14--0.19 F1, and even at $K{=}2$ the GNN
already reads at 0.87. Depth is a multi-hop quantity; message passing computes it and
flat features cannot.

\textbf{The reading is structural, and size-invariant.} To test whether the GNN reads
\emph{structure} or a lookup over node counts, we train on 7-input boolean circuit
graphs and test on 9-input graphs --- an input count never seen during GNN training
(Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}, Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). The graph readouts transfer
essentially losslessly (GCN 0.96, GAT 0.93 on unseen sizes, \emph{above} their
same-size performance); the feature-only baseline does not generalize (0.76, no better
than chance-adjusted same-size reading), because the degree statistics it relies on
shift with input count. Structure is size-invariant; the GNN reads structure.

\textbf{The boundary: architecture.} The same experiment includes a control: graph
networks trained on MLP circuit graphs, tested on \emph{transformer} circuit graphs of
the same task. Every readout --- GCN, GAT, and the feature-only baseline --- collapses
to $\approx$0.32 macro-F1 (chance), because depth-bin semantics and wiring statistics
differ between architectures (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}: 6 vs.\ 7 unit layers, 137 vs.\
250+ nodes, sparsity 0.71 vs.\ 0.42). Structural explicability is therefore
\emph{size-invariant but architecture-specific}: a graph network learns the wiring
\emph{language} of the architecture it was trained on, and reading transfers within that
language, not across it. This is itself a finding --- and it is exactly the kind of
claim that becomes testable only when explicability is a measurable graph property.

\subsection{What graphs cannot read}

We probed one further question: can a graph readout predict \emph{recovery} --- how much
of an unseen target circuit a model learned --- from structure alone? We collected
boolean circuit graphs spanning input counts (5, 7, 9), gate counts, widths, and
trained/untrained weights (recovery F1 ranging 0.40--0.95) and trained readouts on
$\{5,7\}$-input graphs to predict recovery F1 on 9-input graphs, with the ground-truth
circuit never shown (clean protocol in
\texttt{benchmarks/run\_recovery\_control.py}, numbers in
\texttt{results/recovery\_control.json}). All readouts --- GCN, GAT, and the
feature-only baseline --- failed (test $R^2<0$ for every readout: $-4.1$, $-5.9$, and
$-1.8$ for GCN, GAT, and baseline, respectively); the baseline's failure is the
informative one: the barrier is informational, not architectural, because recovery is a
statement about a \emph{target} the extractor never sees, so no function of the
extracted graph alone can certify it.
This is the boundary condition that makes the positive results meaningful: graph
readouts can infer \emph{structural} explicability (roles, depth, density, modularity)
and generalize it across sizes, but \emph{semantic} content --- agreement with an
external specification --- requires comparing against that specification, not reading
the graph. Mechanistic explicability splits cleanly along this line.

\section{Discussion}

GNOmE's thesis is that mechanistic interpretability has been asking the right questions
with the wrong objects. Attention maps, saliency, and patching-based circuits all try to
answer graph questions --- which paths, which dependencies, which modules --- with
non-graph instruments. Once the computation is \emph{extracted as a graph} from real
derivatives, three properties follow. \emph{Measurement} becomes graph statistics with
no ground truth and a composite score; \emph{certification} becomes possible where the
truth is known by construction (recall 1.00 in 9/9 trained boolean models --- trained
networks demonstrably \emph{contain} the wiring of the circuits they compute);
\emph{reading} becomes a graph-network problem, and graph networks demonstrably see what
feature-only models cannot (0.91--0.96 vs.\ 0.72--0.76 macro-F1 on depth roles, with
transfer to unseen sizes). And the framework's testability pays off precisely where
other approaches hand-wave: we can report, with numbers, that the reading is
size-invariant but architecture-specific, and that semantic recovery is not readable
from structure by any method.

The method has limitations worth stating plainly. Recovery is functional (input-pair
co-wiring), not per-parameter identity: a model could in principle implement the same
co-wiring with different gates. Extraction thresholds are per-layer relative; they make
the graph scale-free but introduce a free parameter ($\mathit{rel\_thresh}$, here 0.5)
whose sensitivity we have not exhaustively mapped. The GNN experiments use small,
single-task graphs; scaling reading to the circuit graphs of production-scale language
models is an open problem --- though the extraction itself (blockwise Jacobians) is
precisely the kind of computation that scales with tensor cores. And the
architecture-specificity of reading suggests that \emph{transferable} explicability
models will need either richer structural features or explicit cross-architecture
training.

None of these limits change the central claim. A model's computation is a graph; GNOmE
extracts it, measures it, and learns to read it --- and in doing so converts
mechanistic interpretability from a collection of heuristics into a measurable,
learnable, testable object of study. The graph is not a metaphor for the computation.
It is the computation.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Models and tasks}
\textbf{MLP.} $d_{\text{in}} \to 32 \to 32 \to d_{\text{out}}$ with ReLU, six unit
layers (input, linear, ReLU, linear, ReLU, output). \textbf{Transformer.} One attention
layer plus per-token MLP with residual connections (modular tasks, two one-hot tokens)
or two layers over per-bit tokens (boolean tasks); block list is
embed $\to$ (attention, MLP)${}^{\ell}$ $\to$ readout. All models are trained to 100\%
train accuracy with Adam. \textbf{Boolean tasks.} Random layered DAGs of
\{AND, OR, XOR\} gates over $n_{\text{in}}$ bits, $n_g$ gates, fixed seed; the
ground-truth graph is the literal generated DAG. \textbf{Modular tasks.} $(a+b)\bmod p$
with one-hot inputs; the ground-truth circuit is the canonical Fourier/trigonometric
construction \citep{nanda2022addition}: per-frequency cos/sin features, product terms
implementing the addition formula, a sum, and an argmax readout.

\subsection{Extraction}
For each block $k$ with unit dims $(d_{k{+}1}, d_k)$, estimate
$\mathbf{W}_k[i,j] = \mathbb{E}_x|\partial u_{k{+}1}[i]/\partial u_k[j]|$: closed-form
exact $|\mathbf{W}|$ for linear blocks, diagonal $|f'|$ for elementwise blocks, one
autograd pass per output unit otherwise, averaged over batches of 256. Threshold per
layer at $\mathit{rel\_thresh}\times$ the layer's mean nonzero weight; keep edges above
the threshold. Residual connections are folded into the block they close. Output: a
layered DAG with node ids $u_{k,j}$, layer ranks, and per-edge Jacobian weights.

\subsection{Metrics}
\textbf{Sparsity} $= 1 - |E|/\sum_k d_{k{+}1}d_k$. \textbf{Modularity} via greedy
Louvain communities on the undirected graph, clipped to $[0,1]$.
\textbf{Effective depth} $=$ longest directed path. \textbf{Bottleneck}
$= 1 - \min_k d_k / \sum_k d_k$ (narrowest layer). \textbf{Role diversity} $=$ fraction
of distinct (in-degree, out-degree) signatures. \textbf{MES} $= 0.4\,F_1^{\text{rec}}
+ 0.2\,\text{sparsity} + 0.2\,\text{modularity} + 0.2\,\text{depth fidelity}$ when a
ground truth exists (fidelity $=\min(1, d_{\text{ext}}/d_{\text{gt}})$), else the
recovery weight redistributes onto the other components.
\textbf{Recovery} compares input-pair co-wiring: for each pair of inputs, does some
hidden unit (extracted) / gate (ground truth) depend on both? Precision, recall, F1
over input pairs.

\subsection{Graph networks}
\textbf{GCN} layers: $\mathbf{X}' = \text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}(D^{-1}A\,\mathbf{X}))$ with
edge weights and self-loops; \textbf{GAT} layers: attention
$\alpha_{ij} \propto \exp(\text{LeakyReLU}(\mathbf{a}^\top[\mathbf{h}_i;\mathbf{h}_j]))$
weighted by edge strength, two heads. Three stacked layers, hidden width 64, role head
on node embeddings. \textbf{Node features} (structure-only): normalized out/in degree,
mean outgoing/incoming edge weight --- no layer index. \textbf{Baseline}: MLP on the
same features, no edges. Training: Adam, 300 epochs, cross-entropy on depth bins
(input/early/mid/late/output); few-shot exp1 uses $K\in\{2,4,6\}$ training graphs and
held-out graphs; exp2 trains on six 7-input MLP graphs and evaluates on held-out
same-size, 9-input unseen-size, and transformer graphs.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
All numbers are produced by the committed scripts
(\texttt{benchmarks/run\_benchmark.py}, \texttt{benchmarks/run\_gnn\_experiment.py},
\texttt{benchmarks/analyze.py}) on CPU (PyTorch 2.10, Python 3.14, 8 threads) and
written to committed JSONs (\texttt{results/benchmark\_cpu.json},
\texttt{results/gnn\_experiment.json}); figures are regenerated by
\texttt{benchmarks/make\_figures.py}. Full wall time for both experiments: $\approx$8
minutes on CPU. Nothing is simulated or copied from the literature.
