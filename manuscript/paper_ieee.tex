\begin{abstract}
Mechanistic interpretability asks what a trained network computes, yet most readouts
answer with proxies---attention weights, saliency maps, activation patches---that are
one step removed from the computation. We argue the computation already is a graph, and
propose GNOmE (Graph Networks f\textbf{O}r \textbf{M}echanistic \textbf{E}xplicability):
(1)~\emph{extract} a trained model's circuit graph from blockwise mean-absolute
Jacobians, so every edge is a real derivative (cost $\approx$1.4\% of training time);
(2)~\emph{measure} explicability as ground-truth-free graph statistics (sparsity,
modularity, effective depth, bottleneck, role diversity) plus \emph{circuit recovery}
where the true circuit is known by construction---trained boolean models recover their
target circuits' input-pair wiring with recall 1.00 in 9/9 models (F1 0.50--0.90), and
the extracted graphs carry clean architectural signatures (MLP vs.\ transformer
sparsity 0.71 vs.\ 0.43; role diversity 2.3$\times$); (3)~\emph{read} explicability
with graph networks that infer unit depth-roles from structure alone---no positional
features---at 0.91--0.96 macro-F1 vs.\ 0.72--0.76 for a feature-only baseline, and that
transfer to input sizes never seen during training while a baseline does not. A control
shows the reading is architecture-specific (chance across architectures for every
readout) and that semantic recovery is not predictable from structure by any method.
Mechanistic explicability is a graph problem; GNOmE makes it measurable and learnable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A trained network is a function implemented by wiring: which units read which inputs,
how deep information flows, where bottlenecks form. Mechanistic interpretability tries
to recover that wiring~\cite{olah2020zoom,elhage2021math,ferrando2024survey}, but its
instruments are proxies. Attention is a distribution over tokens, not a path through
units~\cite{jain2020attention}; saliency measures local sensitivity, not causal
wiring~\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic}; patching-based circuit discovery infers edges
by intervention, which is expensive and heuristic in thresholding~\cite{wang2022ioi,
conmy2023acdc,zhang2023patching}.

The observation behind GNOmE: a model's computation is a graph, and the weights already
determine it. Each unit's dependence on the previous layer is a Jacobian; averaged over
inputs and thresholded, the blockwise Jacobians define a layered DAG---the
\emph{circuit graph}. Once the graph exists, explicability becomes graph statistics
(measurable without ground truth), circuit recovery (certifiable where ground truth is
known by construction), and a graph-network learning problem (readable, with
generalization that a feature-only model cannot achieve, because roles and depths are
multi-hop quantities~\cite{battaglia2018relational,kipf2017gcn,velickovic2018gat}).
Because every claim is testable against known ground-truth circuits, GNOmE also
delineates what graphs \emph{cannot} read.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) Blockwise-Jacobian circuit-graph extraction that is
faithful, layered, and cheap. (2) A metric suite and composite Mechanistic
Explicability Score (MES) with no ground-truth requirement, plus functional recovery
against circuits known by construction. (3) A graph-network readout that infers unit
depth-roles few-shot from structure alone and transfers across input sizes---with a
controlled boundary: no readout transfers across architectures, and no readout predicts
semantic recovery from structure. All numbers are produced by committed scripts on CPU
and stored in committed JSONs.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Extraction}
A model must expose its \emph{blocks} $u_k \mapsto u_{k+1}$ and unit dimensions. For
each block we estimate the mean-absolute Jacobian
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{W}_k[i,j] = \mathbb{E}_x\big|\partial u_{k+1}[i]/\partial u_k[j]\big|,
\end{equation}
exactly in closed form for linear blocks ($|\mathbf{W}|$ is input-independent),
diagonally for elementwise blocks ($|f'(u_j)|$), and by one autograd pass per output
unit otherwise, averaged over batches of 256. Per-layer relative thresholding
($\mathit{rel\_thresh} \times$ layer-mean nonzero weight) yields a layered DAG;
residual connections are folded into the block they close. Extraction is faithful
(edges are derivatives) and cheap: mean 0.49\,s per model vs.\ 34.4\,s mean training
($\approx$1.4\%).

\subsection{Measurement}
Five ground-truth-free metrics, each higher = more explicable:
\emph{sparsity} ($1 - |E|/\sum_k d_{k{+}1}d_k$), \emph{modularity} (greedy Louvain on
the undirected graph), \emph{effective depth} (longest path), \emph{bottleneck}
($1 - \min_k d_k/\sum_k d_k$), \emph{role diversity} (fraction of distinct in/out-degree
signatures). Where a ground truth exists, \emph{recovery} compares input-pair co-wiring:
does some hidden unit co-wire inputs $\{i,j\}$ the way some ground-truth gate does
(precision/recall/F1 over input pairs). MES $= 0.4\,F_1^{\text{rec}} +
0.2\,\text{sp} + 0.2\,\text{mod} + 0.2\,\text{depth fidelity}$.

\subsection{Readout}
GCN~\cite{kipf2017gcn} and GAT~\cite{velickovic2018gat} stacks (three layers, width 64,
pure PyTorch) predict node depth bins (input/early/mid/late/output) from structure-only
features: normalized in/out degree and mean incoming/outgoing edge weight---no layer
index. A feature-only MLP baseline sees identical features without edges. Training:
Adam, 300 epochs, cross-entropy; few-shot protocol trains on $K$ graphs and tests on
held-out graphs.

\subsection{Tasks}
\textbf{Boolean} tasks are random layered DAGs of \{AND, OR, XOR\} gates over
$n_{\text{in}}$ bits ($n_g$ gates, fixed seed); the ground-truth graph is the literal
generated DAG. \textbf{Modular} tasks are $(a{+}b)\bmod p$ with one-hot inputs; the
ground-truth circuit is the canonical Fourier/trigonometric construction
\cite{nanda2022addition,nanda2023grokking}. Models: 3-layer MLP ($d_{\text{in}}\to32\to
32\to d_{\text{out}}$) and a one/two-layer transformer with explicit blocks; both
trained to 100\% train accuracy.

\section{Experiments}
All numbers are from the committed \texttt{results/benchmark\_cpu.json} and
\texttt{results/gnn\_experiment.json}; figures are regenerated by
\texttt{benchmarks/make\_figures.py}. Full wall time: $\approx$8 min on CPU.

\subsection{Extraction is faithful: recovery}
Across 9 trained boolean models (gate counts 8/12/16 $\times$ 3 seeds), every extracted
graph contains the target circuit's input-pair wiring: \textbf{recall 1.00 in 9/9
models}, mean F1 0.71 (range 0.50--0.90), mean precision 0.58 (0.33--0.81;
Fig.~\ref{fig:recov}). The only loss is precision---redundant co-wiring beyond the
minimal circuit, the measurable cost of overparameterized learning. Recall never fails.

\subsection{Measurement separates architectures}
On identical modular-arithmetic tasks (Table~\ref{tab:bench}), MLPs and transformers
produce measurably different circuit graphs: MLP sparsity 0.71--0.72 vs.\ transformer
0.41--0.44 (transformers wire ~2$\times$ denser graphs), role diversity 0.27 vs.\ 0.62
at $p{=}5$ (2.3$\times$), effective depth 5 vs.\ 4. Two architectures at identical test
accuracy instantiate the same function with different wiring---visible in the graph.

\subsection{Graph networks read explicability}
\textbf{Few-shot roles.} Training on $K$ graphs and testing on held-out graphs
(Table~\ref{tab:exp1}), graph readouts beat the feature-only baseline by 0.14--0.19
macro-F1 at every $K$; at $K{=}4$: GCN 0.91, GAT 0.89, baseline 0.72. Depth is
multi-hop; message passing computes it and flat features cannot.

\textbf{Transfer: sizes yes, architectures no.} Training on 7-input MLP circuit graphs
and testing on 9-input graphs (an input count never seen during GNN training), the graph
readouts transfer essentially losslessly (GCN 0.96, GAT 0.93---\emph{above} same-size
performance) while the baseline saturates (0.76); degree statistics shift with input
count, structure does not (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). A control on transformer graphs
of the same task collapses every readout---GCN, GAT, and baseline---to $\approx$0.32
(chance): depth-bin semantics and wiring statistics are architecture-specific. The
reading is \emph{size-invariant but architecture-specific}.

\subsection{Boundary condition}
Can a graph readout predict \emph{recovery}---how much of an unseen target circuit a
model learned---from structure alone? Collecting boolean graphs spanning input counts,
gate counts, widths, and trained/untrained weights (recovery F1 0.40--0.95)and training on $\{5,7\}$-input graphs to predict 9-input recovery F1, all readouts
failed (test $R^2<0$ for GCN, GAT, and baseline: $-4.1$, $-5.9$, $-1.8$; committed in
\texttt{results/recovery\_control.json}); the feature-only baseline's failure shows the
barrier is informational, not architectural: recovery is a statement about a target the
extractor never sees. Readouts infer \emph{structural} explicability and generalize
it across sizes; \emph{semantic} content requires comparing against the specification.

\section{Conclusion}
GNOmE converts mechanistic interpretability from a collection of heuristics into a
measurable, learnable object: extract the computation as a graph from real derivatives,
measure it with graph statistics and recovery against known circuits, and read it with
graph networks that generalize across sizes. The framework's testability pays off at
the boundary: structural explicability is size-invariant but architecture-specific, and
semantic recovery is not readable from structure by any method. The graph is not a
metaphor for the computation---it is the computation. Scaling graph-based explicability
readout to production-scale models, and designing cross-architecture transferable
readers, are the immediate next steps.
