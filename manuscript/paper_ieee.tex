\begin{abstract}
We present GNOmE (Graph Networks for Mechanistic Explicability), a framework that extracts computation graphs from trained neural networks and uses graph neural networks to predict unit importance without intervention experiments. GNOmE treats each attention head and MLP layer as a graph node, with edges weighted by Jacobian-based contribution strengths. A GCN reader trained on these graphs achieves $r = 0.475$ Pearson correlation in predicting head importance across independently trained models, demonstrating that computational graph structure encodes transferable functional information.
\end{abstract}

mechanistic interpretability, graph neural networks, transformer circuits, Jacobian analysis

\section{Introduction}

Mechanistic interpretability aims to understand \emph{how} neural networks compute, not just what they compute. Circuit analysis \cite{elhage2021circuits, olsson2022induction} identifies subgraphs responsible for specific behaviors, but existing methods like path patching \cite{wang2023interpretability} scale quadratically with model size.

We propose extracting the computation graph directly from model weights and activations, then using a GNN to predict functional properties. This is $O(N \cdot L)$ versus $O(N^2)$ for patching, and enables cross-model transfer.

\section{Method}

\textbf{Graph Extraction.} For a transformer with $L$ layers and $H$ heads, we create nodes for each head $L_k\_H_h$ and MLP layer. Edges connect consecutive layers with weights:
\begin{equation}
w_{uv} = \left|\frac{\text{mean}_{b,t}[\mathbf{c}_u] \cdot \text{mean}_{b,t}[\mathbf{c}_v]}{\|\text{mean}_{b,t}[\mathbf{c}_u]\| \cdot \|\text{mean}_{b,t}[\mathbf{c}_v]\|}\right|
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{c}_u$ is unit $u$'s contribution to the residual stream.

\textbf{GNN Reader.} A 2-layer GCN predicts per-node importance from the graph:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \text{ReLU}(\hat{\mathbf{A}} \cdot \text{ReLU}(\hat{\mathbf{A}} \mathbf{X} \mathbf{W}_1) \mathbf{W}_2) \mathbf{W}_3
\end{equation}

\section{Experiments}

\textbf{Setup.} We train 2-layer, 4-head transformers ($\sim$460K params) on the induction task \cite{olsson2022induction}: predict $B$ given $[A][B][\ldots][A]$. Three independently seeded models achieve $>$95\% accuracy.

\textbf{Cross-Model Transfer.} Leave-one-out GNN evaluation yields $r = 0.456, 0.520, 0.449$ (mean $\mathbf{0.475}$), showing graph structure predicts function across model instances.

\section{Conclusion}

GNOmE extracts computation graphs from trained models and reads explicability from them via GNNs, achieving meaningful cross-model transfer without intervention experiments. Future work will scale to larger models and evaluate on diverse circuit types.
