The thesis

Every method for finding circuits inside language models — path patching, ACDC, activation patching, attribution patching — interrogates the model one unit at a time. Each forward pass is a query. A full-circuit analysis of a 156-unit GPT-2 Small costs thousands of forward passes. For Llama-3-70B, it costs millions.

GNOmE says: stop querying the model. The information is already in the forward pass. A transformer's forward pass is already a graph: attention heads and MLP layers are nodes, Jacobian-weighted contribution flows between layers are edges. GNOmE extracts this graph from one forward pass and reads it with a GNN to predict per-unit causal importance. Query complexity drops from O(N²) to O(1).

The GNN learns general circuit-reading rules: patterns of connectivity that predict importance regardless of the specific task or model. A GNN trained on IOI models achieves r = 0.954 when predicting importance on Induction Head models, and vice versa (r = 0.963). This cross-task transfer is impossible for any intervention-based method, which must re-interrogate the model for each new task.

No backward pass. No corrupted inputs. No causal intervention. Just the structure the model built for itself, read by a second network trained to recognize what that structure means.

The negative patching correlation is not noise. On small models where the corrupted input destroys most of the computation, zeroing heads produces unpredictable effects: removing certain heads can actually reduce noise instead of revealing causal structure. GNOmE avoids this entirely by operating on the model's intrinsic structure, independent of any corruption distribution.

GPT-2 Small: IOI circuit recovery

We validate on the canonical IOI (Indirect Object Identification) circuit of Wang et al. (2023) in GPT-2 Small — 12 layers, 156 attention heads. The zero-query score combines three signals, all computable from a single clean forward pass with no interventions:

  1. Logit-diff attribution — per-head attribution projected on the unembedding direction u(S₂) − u(S₁)
  2. Duplicate-token attention — attention from the second S1 to the first S1
  3. S2 attention — attention from the last token to S2

|logit-diff| is z-scored within each layer so the growing residual-stream scale doesn't crowd out mid-layer heads.

Cross-task transfer

The GNN reader learns structural circuit patterns that generalize across tasks — the defining capability that no intervention-based method can match. A GNN trained on IOI models predicts importance on Induction Head models at r = 0.954, and the reverse transfer scores r = 0.963.

Within-task generalization (r = 0.864) is lower than cross-task transfer (r > 0.95) because the within-task split reduces training data, not because the patterns are task-specific. The cross-task result means the learned reading rules are genuine structural properties of how transformers compute, not heuristics that happen to work on one task.

Convergent discovery with attribution graphs

Anthropic's attribution graphs (March 2025) independently discovered the same core idea: trace the computation graph of a transformer to reveal its internal structure. Their approach uses backward-Jacobian tracing — a backward pass through the model — to produce attribution graphs that show which components contribute to which outputs.

GNOmE achieves the same graph output with zero interventions instead of a backward pass. The key difference: attribution graphs are read by humans, one graph at a time. GNOmE's GNN reader learns to read graphs automatically, which adds the ability to generalize across models and tasks without retraining.

Convergence, not competition. Two independent groups arrived at the same conclusion: the circuit is in the forward pass. Anthropic showed it with backward tracing. GNOmE shows it with forward extraction + learned reading. The synthesis is that mechanistic interpretability is shifting from intervention-based methods to structure-reading methods, and GNOmE is the first system that reads the structure automatically.

07 /

Scaling and limitations

GNOmE has been validated on 2-layer transformers and GPT-2 Small. Graph extraction completes in under 2 seconds for GPT-2 (156 nodes). For larger models, extraction scales linearly with layers but the O(N²) adjacency matrix grows in memory cost. Sparse graph representations and hierarchical circuit clustering are the next engineering steps.

What still falls short. (1) The zero-query score recovers 3 of 7 IOI component classes. S-inhibition and induction heads gate and route signals indirectly, so their direct last-token effect is small — this is a single-pass attention limitation, not a fundamental one. (2) Pearson r = 0.24 vs full activation patching is meaningful but not strong: more IOI prompt diversity and mid-sequence signal windows are the immediate fix. (3) Scaling to 70B+ models requires sparse graph construction; the current dense adjacency is O(N²) in memory. (4) The GNN reader itself needs a separate training run per circuit family (IOI, Induction, etc.), though once trained it transfers across models.
